 - Human factor, Human-Centred Design
- Ethics application
- Tikanga (Māori) in evaluation protocol

When you test a design on real people you have a duty to protect them. Ethics is the set of rules for that. Explain what you're doing, let people say no, keep them safe, and treat them and their culture with respect.

Principles:
- Informed and voluntary consent.
	Tell people what will happen, let them choose freely. They can stop at any time.
- Privacy and confidentiality
	Don't expose who they are or what they did. Record anonymously (Participant 3)
- Minimisation of risk
	Keep the chance of harm as low as you can.
- Truthfulness and limited deception
	Don't mislead people about the study
- Social and cultural sensivitity, including Treaty of Waitangi
	Respect Maori values and partnership.
- Research adequacy
	Study must be well designed enough to be worth asking people's time. Badly designed study wastes it.
- Avoidance of conflict of interest
	Dont let your own interests bias the results or the people involved.
- Vulnerable participants
	Take care of people who may be more at risk, such as children or older people.
- Respect for property
	Respect the university property and other people's intellectual property.

Ethics application: Application form, Information sheet, Consent form, Flyer.

I must apply these principles into my evaluation when real participants are involved.
