# Rubric
## Group Component
**30%** Low-Fidelity: Wireframe Sketch 
**30%** Low-Fidelity: Wireframe Design 
**40%** High-Fidelity Design: Interactive

- The design is presented clearly.
- Comprehensive coverage of core UI components: UI, dashboard layout, map, and node-link.
- Explicit reference to fundamental HCI principles and design implications with strong justification of applied design techniques and no violations.
- Creative elements are purposeful and enhance usability rather than distract from it.
- Strong coherence with prior research analysis conducted in A1.
- High level of collaboration and shared product ownership, with equal and meaningful contribution from all the team members.

## Individual Component
**70%** Individual contributions
**30%** Lessons learned

- The individual contributed in a valuable way to the project. The individual is also able to articulate the key performance criteria of successful teams and evaluate the group's performance accordingly.
- The individual had a level of engagement that demonstrated a strong commitment to the class and the learning outcomes. The voice of the individual writer is evident.

# Submission
1. Group cover sheet
2. Contribution report combining the individual reports of each team member, covering:
	1. . Each member’s own contributions
	2. Each member’s own lessons learned
3. Group PowerPoint file, including
	1. 1 slide for project background
	2. Slides for sketches and explanations
	3. Slides for wireframes and explanations
	4. Slides for high-fidelity designs and explanations
	5. 1 slide for UI interface walkthrough (in a video)
	6. 1 slide containing a table with mutual evaluation of contributions to the group project and shared output, including:
		1. Team member name and student ID
		2. Member ratings of each individual’s contribution (expressed as a percentage, e.g., 20% out of 100%)
		3. Notes supporting each rating or flagged issues
4. Group Figma design project access
5. Group presentation recording: a video of your group presentation and demonstration of the final interactive design solution in action.
	1. Ensure the file size and video length are reasonable, the resolution is clear, and no sped-up playback is used.
	2. The team arranges the speaking order so that each member has an opportunity to speak
