•A fishbone diagram (or Ishikawa/cause-and-effect diagram) is a visual tool for brainstorming and categorising potential root causes of a specific problem or effect, shaped like a fish skeleton

•Helping teams systematically uncover why something happens by branching causes into major categories (like People, Method, Equipment, Environment) and drilling down with techniques like the 5W1H
![[Pasted image 20260819105301.png]]

- Step 1: Add details to your fishbone analysis. Keep asking why to further identify sub-causes that contribute to a problem
- Step 2: Brainstorm all possible causes. Ask why this problem occurred, and organise possible causes by category
- Step 3: Label potential issues. You can use the six labels in the classic fishbone diagram
- Step 4: Define the problem. Create a clear, concise problem statement. This should address a known issue or one you’re trying to prevent
- Step 5: Review each cause and develop action items. 

Problem Statement Formula: User + Need = Goal
- As a [user], I need/want [need] so that [goal]
- People order food when they feel overwhelmed or don’t have enough time in their day to cook. Ordering a prepared meal allows them to eat quickly without having to spend time shopping for groceries, cooking a meal, or doing dishes afterwards.
- As a [person], I want [to be given food] so that [I have more time]
- Be specific, not too broad or general: As a [25-year-old single adult], I want [to call a restaurant] so that [I don’t have to cook meals].
- As a [working adult], I want [to order prepared food] so  [I have time for other things].
- Be empathetic: As a [busy, working adult], I want [to spend less time thinking about my food] so [I spend my time on other things instead].

Combine with a How Might We statement
